# Swarm Studio stories

Run 1, 2026-11-09, made with the `human-stories` skill. Five picks, then the other survivors and the dropped ones.

**Brief.** Swarm Studio, a plugin for Hermes. Commander Hermia's crew builds a whole launch from one prompt (a multi-page site, a live app with a database, a playable game, a short film, posts) on a canvas where you watch their cursors move. You steer by talking or by editing on the canvas yourself, and you can post to Swarm Feed, where people try builds live and remix them with credit. It runs on your own Hermes with your own models.

**Uses.** Home page persona strips (Before / Moment / After), one hero story slide in the pitch deck, and the demo video's 6-second opening problem beat with one before line on screen.

**Numbers.** None. No line below uses a count, clock time or statistic, so there is no sources section.

**Picture rule.** Each beat centres one face, the person's own, with the lower third left clear for the line. Anyone else is hands, a back or a blur. Any screen in frame shows a real Swarm build, never a mock-up ([BRAND.md](BRAND.md), "Real output only").

## Suggested placement

- **Home page strips:** Odalys (design), Teodor (game), Citlali (Swarm Feed).
- **Deck hero slide:** Paloma. One sentence to Hermia changes every piece of the launch at once, which is the pitch in a single scene.
- **Video opening beat:** "His daughter keeps asking to play his game. There isn't one."
- **Spare:** Brennan, for the live-app section or a later ad.

## 1. Teodor

**Who:** early forties, a pixel artist who has drawn for other people's games for years.
**Task:** a playable demo of his own game, a mole who carries letters through tunnels, for an indie festival that closes on Friday.

**Before line:** His daughter keeps asking to play his game. There isn't one.
**Quote:** "Heavier. Like his bag's full of letters."

| Beat | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Before | All he had was a gray square that fell through the floor. | Night, kitchen table. Teodor's face lit by a laptop where a gray square drops off the screen; a sketchbook of mole drawings at his elbow. Face in the upper half, dark table below. |
| Moment | He presses Play, and his mole digs the tunnels he described. | Close on his face leaning in as he presses Play, the canvas glow on his glasses, crew cursors and tunnels soft behind. Lower third clear. |
| After | His daughter plays it on the couch and won't give it back. | Living room. His daughter's back and the laptop on her knees, out of focus in front; Teodor's face behind her, watching. Plain cushion below. |

<details>
<summary>Private scene</summary>

Teodor's drawings are in other people's games: doors, birds, a city under the sea. His name sits low in end titles that scroll past too fast to read. His own game lives in a folder called "someday": a mole who carries letters through tunnels under a sleeping town. The indie festival closes submissions on Friday, and it wants a link a judge can click and play.

He followed a tutorial series until the lesson on collisions, where his mole, still a gray square, fell through the floor and kept falling. He tried a game-making app, and everything he built came out looking like its sample game. He asked Bogdan, who coded their game jams years ago. Bogdan said "send me the sprites this weekend," and then his son was born, and Teodor is not going to ask a man with a newborn again.

His daughter Oana keeps asking when she can play "your one." She has only ever seen his drawings inside games she's too young for.

He types the mole, the tunnels and the letters into Swarm Studio and watches the crew lay the tunnels out on the canvas. He presses Play. The mole digs. The jump is floaty, wrong for a mole with a full bag. "Heavier," he tells Hermia. "Like his bag's full of letters." He presses Play again, and it's right.

Oana plays it on the couch with her tongue between her teeth and won't give it back. He sends Bogdan a link with no favor in it: play it when the baby sleeps.

</details>

## 2. Paloma

**Who:** late twenties, the only marketer at her older sister's coffee-grinder startup.
**Task:** get the site, the waitlist app, the film and the posts to match the red on the new box before launch.

**Before line:** Her sister quit her job for this box. Nothing else matched it.
**Quote:** "Make the red match the box. Deeper, like dried chili."

| Beat | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Before | The site, the waitlist and the posts lived in different places, all in the old red. | Warehouse, cold light. Paloma's face over a laptop; at the frame edge her sister's hand holds up the red box. Concrete floor below. |
| Moment | She tells Hermia to match the box, and every piece changes at once. | Paloma's face as she speaks to the laptop; on screen the site, the waitlist app and the posts sit side by side on the canvas, turning the box's red. Lower third clear. |
| After | She turns the laptop to her sister, who laughs for the first time all week. | Paloma's face, half a smile, looking past the turned laptop toward the laugh. The box on the table below. |

<details>
<summary>Private scene</summary>

The first boxes come back from the printer, and Graciela holds one up under the warehouse lights. "This," she says. "This is the red." It's deeper than anything Paloma has made for the launch, and the launch is next week.

The site is with a freelancer who's at a wedding with his phone off. The waitlist lives in another service, behind a settings page she has to hunt for every time. The promo film is with an editor who's booked solid and would need a new quote. The launch posts are a folder of exports she made by hand, one at a time, in the old red. She spends the evening re-exporting and writing polite emails and has nothing she can show.

Graciela quit a hospital job to make grinders. Her name is printed on the side of the box. Last month, when Paloma said it was getting to be a lot, Graciela said, "You told me you could handle it." She wasn't wrong. That's the part that stings.

Paloma types the whole launch into Swarm Studio once: the grinder, the box, the waitlist, the film, the posts. The crew builds them side by side on one canvas. Then she tells Hermia, "Make the red match the box. Deeper, like dried chili." The site, the waitlist app and every post turn at once.

She turns the laptop around. Graciela looks for a long second, then laughs, the first time all week. Paloma gets home before the last bus.

</details>

## 3. Citlali

**Who:** nineteen, a first-year graphic design student who works her grandmother's tamale stand on Saturdays.
**Task:** a small website about the stand for her class's end-of-term show.

**Before line:** Her grandmother still asks when she'll pick a real major.
**Quote:** "Look, abuela. That's my name on it."

| Beat | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Before | Her cousin built her a page of black text and said design comes last. | First light at the market, steam. Citlali's face behind the stand, sketchbook open on the cooler; her grandmother's hands folding husks at the frame edge. Plain counter below. |
| Moment | She posts it to Swarm Feed, and a stranger remixes it with her name on top. | Her face lit by her phone; on it, a Swarm Feed card of someone's remix with her name as the original maker. Lower third clear. |
| After | Her grandmother reads the name and asks for the link to show the next stall. | Citlali's face watching her grandmother, seen from behind, holding the phone close. The stand's counter below. |

<details>
<summary>Private scene</summary>

Citlali thinks in spreads. Her sketchbook is full of her abuela's tamale stand at the Saturday market: the steam, the cooler with the hand-painted sign, the line of men in work boots before it's light. The end-of-term show takes anything, and she wants a small website you walk through like the stand's morning, first steam to last tamale.

She has never built a website. She watched a beginner video and got stuck installing something whose name she couldn't pronounce. Her cousin Ulises, who studies computer science, built her a page in an afternoon: black text, white background, a list of prices. "Design comes last, prima," he said, kindly. She tried a free template, and the stand came out looking like a law office.

Her abuela folds husks and asks, most Sundays, when Citlali is going to pick a real major. She asks it with love. It lands anyway.

Citlali describes the stand in Swarm Studio page by page and watches the crew lay out the morning as spreads, the way she pictured it. She fixes the steam on one page herself. Then she posts it to Swarm Feed. By the next morning someone she's never met has remixed it into their grandfather's shoe-repair stall, and at the top it says it started with her.

She shows her abuela on her phone between customers. Her abuela reads the name slowly, then asks for the link so she can show the woman who sells elotes at the next stall.

</details>

## 4. Brennan

**Who:** mid thirties, a sign painter who runs a roller-skate night.
**Task:** a site, posts and tickets that hold up at the door for the night's reopening at a new rink.

**Before line:** Last time, Casey had a ticket and still got turned away.
**Quote:** "Casey. You're on the list. Go skate."

| Beat | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Before | The reservations, the payments and the door list never matched. | His sign bench, gold leaf, a printed list in his hand with names crossed out. Brennan's face in the upper half, the bench below. |
| Moment | At the door, he opens the ticket app the crew built and finds Casey by name. | Rink entrance at night, his face lit by his phone, coloured lights and wheels blurred behind. Dark doorway below. |
| After | Casey skates past the DJ booth and points at him. | Brennan's face tipped up toward the ceiling, smiling, rink lights above; a skater's blur crossing the frame. Rink floor below. |

<details>
<summary>Private scene</summary>

Brennan letters shop windows for a living, gold leaf and a steady hand, and every flyer for his roller-skate night is painted by him. The old rink closed. The night reopens at a bigger one next month, and it needs a site, tickets that hold up at the door, and posts that look like his flyers.

Last time, people reserved on a free event page and paid through a separate link, and the two never agreed. His co-organizer kept a spreadsheet to match them up, and it drifted a little more each week. On the night, the printed list at the door was wrong.

Casey had a ticket. Casey bought it the week they came out to their parents, and the rink's staff turned them away because the name wasn't on the list, while Brennan was inside on the decks. He found out the next day. He still pictures Casey walking back to the bus stop with skates over a shoulder.

He describes the night to the crew, the gold and black of his flyers, the music, the rink, and they build the site, the posts and a ticket app with a real guest list behind it. On reopening night he stands at the door himself and opens the app on his phone.

He finds Casey by name and taps them in. "Casey. You're on the list. Go skate." Casey skates past the DJ booth and points at him, and Brennan has to look at the ceiling for a second.

</details>

## 5. Odalys

**Who:** mid thirties, a freelance brand designer working out of a converted garage.
**Task:** a bakery client's holiday pre-order site, live by Monday.

**Before line:** Her ex built every site she designed. His number's still saved.
**Quote:** "You keep going. I've got the photo."

| Beat | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Before | A black window asked which framework she wanted, and she shut the laptop. | Cold garage studio. Odalys's face, sleeves over her knuckles, her phone face-up beside her with a half-typed text; finished frames glow on a second screen. Desk below. |
| Moment | The crop is wrong, so she drags the frame while the crew keeps building. | Her face, focused; on screen her cursor pulls a photo frame wider while the crew's cursors keep moving across the order page. Lower third clear. |
| After | She deletes the text to her ex and sends the bakery a live link. | Her face relaxed in the orange glow of a space heater, thumb resting on her phone. Desk below. |

<details>
<summary>Private scene</summary>

The garage is cold enough that Odalys works with her sleeves pulled over her knuckles. On her second screen the bakery's pages are done and lovely: guava pastries on butcher paper, an order page with pickup slots for the holidays. They've been done for weeks. They don't do anything.

She exported them with a plugin, and on a phone the photos stacked like dropped plates. She tried a site builder and lost an afternoon to a margin. A developer friend swore by a coding tool, so she opened it, and a black window filled with text and asked which framework she wanted. She typed "the one that looks good" and shut the laptop.

For years Leo built everything she designed. Leo is her ex. His number is still in her favorites, and she has typed "quick favor" and deleted it more than once.

The bakery's owner has been telling customers at the register that pre-orders open Monday. He printed a sign and taped it to the glass case.

She types the bakery into Swarm Studio the way she'd brief a builder, and the crew's cursors start laying out pages on a canvas she knows how to read. The hero crops the pastries badly. She grabs the frame and drags it wider, and the cursors around hers keep building the order page as if she's one of the crew. "You keep going. I've got the photo."

She deletes the draft to Leo, sends the bakery the live link, and listens to the heater tick.

</details>
